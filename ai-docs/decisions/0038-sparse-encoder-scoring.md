# 0038. Sparse (hierarchical) k-WTA encoder scoring for the next scale order

- **Status:** proposed — direction decided, implementation deliberately not started. As of 2026-08-15 all three prerequisites are resolved (1/2 negative-at-current-scale since 2026-07-15; 3 numerically-validated-with-a-caveat since 2026-08-15, gaps.md §42) — **deferred, not closed**, pending the explicit trigger recorded under prerequisite 2, and pending a growth-campaign-scale confirmation of `per_region_mse` behavior noted in prerequisite 3.
- **Date:** 2026-07-14

## Context

The 10x growth campaign (engineering-log.md §16/§20) grows each region's `KWtaLayer` from `N_UNITS=1024` toward ~9,660 units (honeybee-order, `brain-scale-comparison.md`). Looking for genuine scaling levers toward mouse-order (another ~1,300x) rather than just more GPU cycles, engineering-log.md §25 found and fixed an 11.4× binding-projection speedup by exploiting k-WTA output sparsity.

That fix doesn't extend to k-WTA itself. `KWtaLayer::forward` has two costs: **scoring** (`region.rs:117`, `z=Wx`, dense `[d_input]→[n_units]`, `O(d_input×n_units)`) and **selection** (`region.rs:151`, `O(n_units)` partition, cheap by comparison). Sparsity is a property of the *output* (k of n_units survive), not the input projection — every unit's score must be computed before top-k knows the winners. `scaled_k` keeps k/n≈2% constant as regions grow, controlling downstream FLOPs (§25's target), but the scoring matmul stays `O(d_input×n_units)` regardless of k. A 10x region-size increase is a 10x scoring-cost increase under current all-to-all connectivity — linear, not the sub-linear scaling biological cortical columns get from local receptive fields. Full survey: `research/sparse-coding.md` §"Encoder-side scaling."

## Decision

**Direction: hierarchical/coarse-to-fine WTA** if/when implemented — a cheap coarse scorer over super-units (groups of `block_size`, each with a small summary projection) selects promising blocks, full-resolution scoring runs only within those — reduces `O(d_input×n_units)` to `O(d_input×n_blocks)+O(d_input×block_size×n_selected)`.

Chosen over the other two surveyed mechanisms: preserves an *exact* top-k guarantee within selected blocks (unlike sampling), so it doesn't silently break growth's assumption that a new unit's residual correlation is actually measured; simpler failure mode than MoE-style learned routing (a bad coarse scorer degrades toward "more blocks, less speedup," not toward silently starving regions the way a bad router could); this codebase already has ANN-index machinery (HNSW) that may be directly reusable or informative for the coarse stage rather than needing a bespoke gating network built from scratch.

**Implementation explicitly NOT started** — this ADR records the chosen direction so a future session doesn't re-litigate the three-way comparison, not a commitment to build it.

## Prerequisites before implementation (all 3 resolved; still not started)

1. **Quantify the actual cost — RESOLVED 2026-07-15 (engineering-log.md §28).** The exported ONNX encoder graph has the identical `O(d_input×n_units)` dense matmul as `burn` — no sparsity/gating before scoring. Live wake-path cost at ~9,400-9,493 units/region (`OrtRunEncoder`): ~1.28ms/call mean, small and not even the dominant ORT cost — `OrtRunBinding` costs 2-3× more/call at this scale. **Correction (2026-08-12, §30/ADR 0040):** `OrtRunBinding` was mischaracterized as "fixed-size, region-count-independent" — the GRU/attention nodes are, but the graph also ran a dense region-projection matmul scaling with n_units, 75-76% of `OrtRunBinding`'s own cost at campaign scale. Since replaced by a sparse-gather rewrite (ADR 0040) — the finding (binding costs more than encoder) still holds, by a smaller, gather-reduced margin. Evidence for (not proof of) the "ORT/GPU already absorbs the wasted FLOPs" invalidation condition at *current* scale — doesn't resolve prerequisite 2.
2. **Confirm this matters at the targeted scale — RESOLVED 2026-07-15 (engineering-log.md §29).** Doesn't, yet. engineering-log.md §16 states the campaign's own goal as honeybee-order (~9,660 units/region), not mouse-order, and no documented roadmap item targets going past it. Combined with prerequisite 1 (scoring cost small, non-dominant): hierarchical WTA isn't worth building for the campaign actually running. **"Doesn't matter yet," not "wrong direction"** — stays `proposed`/deferred with an explicit trigger (CLAUDE.md's items-A-N pattern), not "closed." Trigger: campaign target moves past honeybee-order, or the encoder/binding cost balance shifts back toward the encoder mattering.
3. **Numerical validation — RESOLVED 2026-08-15 (gaps.md §42), positive with a caveat.** A minimal block-local scorer (`somnus-perception::region::hierarchical_validation`, not wired into any production path) was built and property/unit-tested against exactly the invariants named above:
   - **k/n sparsity ratio preserved and indices stay in-range/sorted** under block-local selection — proptest-covered across randomized `n`/`block_size`/`k`/`n_blocks_select`.
   - **Duty-cycle stays bounded** ([0,1]) over 2000 rounds, both at the unit level (existing invariant) and a newly-needed **block level** (see caveat).
   - **Newly grown units are immediately eligible** for block-local selection, matching `select_top_k`'s existing growth guarantee.
   - **Caveat — a naive port starves chronically-low-scoring blocks; the fix needs a second, block-level duty-cycle boost, not just the existing per-unit one.** A block that's rarely chosen at the coarse stage never gets *any* of its units selected regardless of their individual duty-cycle boost — the per-unit boost only matters once a block is already selected. The validation module tracks a `block_duty_cycle` (mirroring `duty_cycle` but at block granularity) and boosts coarse block-selection scores by it, same `BOOST_STRENGTH`/`DUTY_CYCLE_ALPHA` constants. With that fix, a block scoring modestly (not arbitrarily) below its peers does recover — but only on the **same ~1000+-round timescale** `DUTY_CYCLE_ALPHA=1/1000` already implies for unit-level fairness in production (a 500-round test run showed zero recovery; a 4000-round run did). This bound is inherent to duty-cycle boosting in general (unit-level or block-level) — it can resolve a raw-score gap up to roughly `BOOST_STRENGTH × target_dc`, not an arbitrarily large one. Not new information about the existing single-stage `select_top_k`, but block-local routing adds a *second* place (block selection, not just unit selection) where this bound now applies, and a naive implementation that only reused per-unit boosting would silently drop that second application — this is exactly the kind of thing this prerequisite existed to catch.
   - **Not validated:** `per_region_mse` trend at growth-campaign scale (engineering-log.md §16-style). The property tests above validate the selection *mechanism's* invariants in isolation; confirming reconstruction quality still improves the way it does under global competition needs a real growth-campaign-scale run, out of scope for this pass — this is the one piece of prerequisite 3 that would still need a pilot before ADR 0038 could move past `proposed`.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Local/blocked connectivity via learned routing (MoE-style gating).** Closest biological analogue, most flexible, but a bad router reintroduces the dead-unit problem this codebase already fought once, and routing quality itself is harder to validate than a fixed coarse-to-fine scorer. Not primary — not ruled out if coarse-to-fine's simpler router proves insufficiently selective.
2. **Approximate top-k via sampling/sketching.** Cheapest, weakest guarantee — units outside the sample can never win even if they'd score highest. Rejected as primary (breaks the exact-top-k assumption growth's residual-correlation logic depends on); could be a cheaper fallback if hierarchical WTA's coarse stage proves too expensive itself.
3. **Do nothing; rely on ORT/MIGraphX constant-factor speedups.** Valid short-term (what's happening now) — ORT handles the current campaign's dense cost adequately. Rejected long-term: constant-factor GPU speedups don't change the linear-in-n_units scaling law, they only push out when it starts dominating.

## Consequences

No code changes from this ADR — exists so the direction (hierarchical/coarse-to-fine) doesn't need re-deriving. Whoever implements this should first produce the scoring-cost benchmark (prerequisite 1) and re-check "what would invalidate this," since the direction was picked from a first-pass survey, not a benchmark-informed comparison. If adopted, interacts with and needs re-validation against `grow_region_seeded`, duty-cycle boosting, and the binding area's assumption that any of a region's n_units can be part of the active set for a given input.

## What would invalidate this

- **Confirmed 2026-07-15 (prerequisites 1/2):** the scoring matmul isn't currently a bottleneck — small in absolute terms, not the dominant ORT cost at honeybee-order scale, the project's actual current target. Does **not** mean the direction was wrong or mouse-order ambitions are abandoned — see prerequisite 2 and engineering-log.md §29 for why this stays deferred, not closed. Only mark "closed" if a future session affirmatively establishes honeybee-order as a decided ceiling (not merely that no next campaign is scoped yet) — a judgment call, not derivable from doc silence alone.
- The live benchmark shows ORT/MIGraphX already absorbs the exported encoder graph's scoring cost well enough (sub-linear in practice via GPU parallelism) that a software-side hierarchical scheme adds complexity without a measurable win — GPU matmul throughput absorbs "wasted" FLOPs a CPU implementation couldn't.
- A simpler mechanism (static block assignment at growth time, no learned/dynamic routing) captures most of the benefit — hierarchical WTA's coarse-scorer stage might be over-engineering if blocks never need re-routing.
- The research direction moves away from region-scaling entirely (same transitive condition ADR 0023/0037 carry).
